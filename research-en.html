📌 Research Interest

Competency Development for AI Doctoral Students
Through Experiential Learning

Research question: How can AI doctoral students develop professional competencies beyond academic skills through structured project-based experiential learning?

Based on first-hand observation at Beijing Zhongguancun Academy · Zhongguancun Institute of Artificial Intelligence, I propose the CORE framework — integrating Competency-Based Education (CBE) and Kolb's Experiential Learning Theory to bridge the gap between academic training and professional competency.

Experiential Learning Competency-Based Education AI Doctoral Training Project-Based Learning
Research Problem

Why This Question?

Four observations that led to the CORE framework.

Observation

Structural Gap in AI Doctoral Training

AI evolves monthly, but doctoral training cycles span years. Students master model accuracy — yet lack project delivery, team collaboration, and cross-domain transfer competencies demanded by industry.

Discovery

Hidden Growth in Experiential Learning

AI research environments — experimental iterations, open-source contributions, conference deadlines, team collaborations — inherently provide rich competency development opportunities. Yet they lack intentional design: growth happens by accident, not system.

Framework

Introducing the CORE Framework

Rooted in CBE and Kolb's experiential learning theory, CORE reframes research projects as structured competency development contexts — helping AI doctoral students deliberately construct professional competencies alongside academic output.

Value

Transferable & Verifiable

While AI doctoral students are the primary application context, CORE's logic transfers to engineering, data science, and other research-intensive disciplines. The framework includes operationalized competency definitions, reflection protocols, and assessment tools.

Research Question

Central Question & Hypotheses

Central Research Question

How do AI doctoral students intentionally develop professional competencies beyond academic skills through structured project experiences?

This study proposes that by intentionally designing AI doctoral project experiences based on the CORE framework, measurable gains can be achieved in collaborative practice, self-management, and professional communication — thereby bridging the structural gap between academic training and industry competency demands.

Q1

Does the competency gap exist?

What systematic gaps exist between the competencies AI doctoral students develop through academic training and those expected by industry?

Q2

How do project experiences drive development?

Can structured project experiences (collaborative delivery, iterative reflection, cross-team coordination) promote AI doctoral students' practical competency development?

Q3

Is the CORE framework effective?

Can the CORE framework be operationally embedded into AI doctoral training to produce measurable competency improvements?

CORE

Making Research Projects the Vehicle for Competency Development

CORE integrates Competency-Based Education (CBE) with Kolb's Experiential Learning Theory. Through four coordinated components, it helps doctoral students develop professional competencies beyond academic skills while conducting research. Core logic: embedding Kolb's experiential cycle into project structure — so every experimental iteration becomes a complete learning cycle: concrete experience → reflection → conceptualization → application.

🧭

Competency
Definition & Mapping

Six competency domains: disciplinary knowledge, research methods, collaborative practice, self-management, reflective practice, professional communication

🔩
